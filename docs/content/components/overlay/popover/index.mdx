The `<Popover>` component is a floating panel anchored to a trigger. Unlike a menu, it is not limited to commands: it holds content people work with in place, including rows with their own controls, and stays open until they dismiss it. On small screens it opens as a tray from the bottom of the screen.

<Callout title="Look for a dedicated component first" type="info">
  Select, Menu, ComboBox, DatePicker, Tooltip and ContextualHelp open a panel
  like this one too, with the right keyboard behaviour and screen reader
  support built in. Reach for `<Popover>` only when none of them fits, for
  example when the panel combines several things or its rows carry their own
  controls.

See [which component to use instead](#dedicated-components-to-use-instead)
for each need.

</Callout>

## Anatomy

A popover opens below its trigger by default. It is composed of an optional header with a title and a description, an optional search row, and the content.

<PopoverAnatomy />

- **Trigger:** The button that opens the popover.
- **Popover:** The floating panel itself, placed next to its trigger.
- **Title:** Optional heading at the top of the panel. It becomes the panel's accessible name.
- **Description:** Optional short text under the title. Together they form the header.
- **Search:** Optional row above the content that filters the list in it.
- **Content:** The body of the panel.

## Usage

A popover lets people deal with something without leaving where they are. The panel opens right next to the button it belongs to, the page stays in view around it, and clicking away or pressing Escape puts everything back as it was. Opening it commits people to nothing. Compared to the alternatives, it saves a trip to another page, it doesn't interrupt like a dialog, and it doesn't push the page around like content that expands inline.

That makes a popover the right place for content that is:

- **About its trigger.** The content belongs to one thing on the page, like the notifications behind a bell or the members behind a "Manage team" button. Showing it next to that thing keeps the connection obvious.
- **Secondary.** Most people can finish their task without it and open it only when they need it, so the page stays uncluttered.
- **Quick to deal with.** People look at something or change something small and move on. Closing the panel loses nothing.

If the content fails one of these, it belongs somewhere else. Information most people need, and input they have to give, belongs on the page. People can miss a popover, and they can close it by accident with one click outside. Content about the page as a whole, or work people want to keep open while they use the page, like a filter panel, belongs in a [`<Drawer>`](/components/overlay/drawer). A decision people have to make before they go on belongs in a [`<Dialog>`](/components/overlay/dialog). A list of commands that run and close, like an account menu, is a [`<Menu>`](/components/overlay/menu).

<GuidelineTiles>
  <Do>
    <DoDescription>
      Use a popover for optional content about one button, like a notifications
      panel or a team list people manage in place.
    </DoDescription>
  </Do>
  <Dont>
    <DontDescription>
      Don't hide what most people need behind a popover. If the content is

  </Dont>

### Details on demand

People scan a list or a table and want to know what is behind one number, like how the ticket sales of an event split up by category. Showing that breakdown on the page would crowd it for everyone, and opening a new page would lose their place. A popover shows the breakdown right next to the number, with a link to go deeper, and disappears again with the next click.

<ComponentDemo name="popover-basic" />

The details are data about the entry, laid out as rows, with a link or a button to act on them. That is what sets this apart from a [`<ContextualHelp>`](/components/overlay/contextual-help), which explains a control in a sentence or two. If the content is a help text, use ContextualHelp. Keep the popover to a few rows. When people need the whole record, with its history, related items or edit options, the popover becomes a page in disguise. Use a [`<Drawer>`](/components/overlay/drawer) or a page instead.

### Rows people act on in place

Some panels are lists where each row has its own actions, like notifications people archive or mark as read. A menu can't hold this, because its rows are commands and can't contain controls. Put a [`<ListView>`](/components/collection/listview) in the popover instead. Each row can carry its own buttons and an action menu, and people can deal with several rows without the panel closing.

<ComponentDemo name="popover-notifications" />

Put the list directly in `<Popover.Content>`. Its dividers then reach the popover's edges while the row text lines up with the title, and the list sits flush at the bottom of the panel. For other edge-to-edge content, like an [`<Accordion>`](/components/navigation/accordion), set `bleed` on `<Popover.Content>`.

### Finding and managing items in a list

When the list gets longer, people need to find the row before they can act on it, like a team member whose role they want to change. Put a `<Popover.Search>` above the content to filter the list as people type. The search takes focus when the panel opens, and Tab moves on into the results.

<ComponentDemo name="popover-search" />

Search belongs in a popover only when people act on the rows in place. To let people type and pick one value, use an [`<Autocomplete>`](/components/form/autocomplete) or a [`<ComboBox>`](/components/form/combobox) instead. The field has no visible label, so give it an `aria-label` and a placeholder that both say what is searched. Under a `<Title>`, the search sits between two dividers. Without one it becomes the popover's header, and the popover then needs an `aria-label`.

### Quick, optional input

Sometimes people want to add a small detail without leaving what they are doing, like a note on an order. A popover holds one to three optional fields right next to the thing they belong to. Put the fields in a [`<Form>`](/components/form/form) inside `<Popover.Content>` and close the popover from its submit handler. Enter submits the form from a single-line field. In a text area it adds a line, so always give the form a Save button. A button with `slot="close"` closes the popover without submitting, which suits Cancel.

<ComponentDemo name="popover-mini-form" />

A popover closes when people click away, and that must never cost them anything. Either the input is so cheap that losing it doesn't matter, or the popover saves as people go. Closing the panel must never submit or delete anything. If dismissing it would lose work or leave people stuck, use a [`<Dialog>`](/components/overlay/dialog).

    <DoDescription>
      Use a popover for one to three optional fields, where dismissing the panel
      loses nothing.
    </DoDescription>
  </Do>
  <Dont>
    <DontDescription>
      Don't use a popover for required input, blocking validation, several steps
      or a destructive confirmation. Use a Dialog.
    </DontDescription>
  </Dont>
</GuidelineTiles>

### Popover vs Menu

A menu is a choice. A popover is a place. In a menu every row is a command: the user picks one, it runs, and the menu closes. In a popover the user stays and works with whatever is inside.

Ask one question to decide: after the user does the main thing inside, should the panel close on its own? If yes, use a menu. If no, use a popover.

There is also a hard rule. If any row contains its own interactive control, such as a switch, a second button, a link or an input, it can't be a menu. Screen readers do not expose controls inside a menu item, and keyboard focus never reaches them.

| Question                                                     | Menu                                               | Popover                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Is every row a command?                                      | Yes                                                | —                                                            |
| Does it close after one selection?                           | Yes                                                | No, it stays open                                            |
| Can rows contain their own controls?                         | No                                                 | Yes                                                          |
| Can it hold any layout, like text, sections and form fields? | No                                                 | Yes                                                          |
| Keyboard                                                     | One tab stop, arrow keys between items, type-ahead | Tab moves through the controls inside                        |
| Selecting rows                                               | Built in with `selectionMode`                      | Put a [`<ListView>`](/components/collection/listview) inside |

Toggles are where this gets mixed up. When the row itself is the toggle, like showing or hiding table columns, use a menu with selection. When each row is content that happens to have a switch, like a notification you can mute, use a popover with a list inside, so the switch is reachable.

<GuidelineTiles>
  <Do>
    <DoDescription>
      Use a popover when rows carry their own controls or the user needs to stay
      and work in the panel.
    </DoDescription>
  </Do>
  <Dont>
    <DontDescription>
      Don't put switches, buttons or links inside menu items. Screen reader
      users can't reach them.
    </DontDescription>
  </Dont>
</GuidelineTiles>

### Popover vs Drawer and Dialog

All three open on top of the current page, so people keep their place. They differ in what the content is about and in how much it asks of people.

|                   | Popover                                | Drawer                                       | Dialog                                    |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| The content is    | about one element, the trigger         | about what is on screen, like a record       | a task or decision people started         |
| It opens          | next to its trigger                    | at the side of the page                      | in the middle of the screen               |
| The page behind   | stays visible, a click there closes it | stays visible and usable                     | is blocked until people respond           |
| It asks of people | nothing, they can ignore it            | light work, up to about five fields          | a response before they go on              |
| It closes         | by clicking away or pressing Escape    | when people close it                         | when people confirm, cancel or close it   |
| Typical content   | a short list, a few details, one field | a record's details, a filter panel, comments | a confirmation, a required form, a choice |

A quick way to decide: if people should keep the panel open while they work on the page, use a drawer. If they must respond before they go on, use a dialog. If they look, change something small and move on, use a popover.

### Modality

A popover is light to look at but modal for keyboard and screen reader users. Both matter, and they don't contradict each other.

For people who see the screen, a popover doesn't block anything. There is no backdrop, the page stays visible around the panel, and a click outside or Escape closes it. This is what makes a popover cheap to open and cheap to leave.

For keyboard and screen reader users, the panel is modal while it is open. Focus stays inside, the rest of the page is hidden from assistive technology, and the page doesn't scroll underneath. Without this, people who can't see the panel lose track of where they are: their reading position can drift onto the page while the panel is still open, and tabbing past the last control closes it without warning. Popovers that hold buttons, links or fields, which is almost every popover, need this. It follows the [React Aria guidance](https://react-spectrum.adobe.com/react-aria/Popover.html) and the [dialog pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/) of the WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices.

Set `nonModal` only for a panel without focusable controls, like a short piece of read-only information. People can then keep using and scrolling the page while it is open. A non-modal popover closes when people click outside of it, move focus elsewhere, press Escape or scroll the page. On small screens the panel opens as a tray, which is always modal.

### Placement and size

Use `placement` to position the panel relative to its trigger, for example `bottom end` for a panel opened from an icon in the top right corner. React Aria flips the panel when there is not enough room. Set `size` to `small`, `medium` or `large` to match the content. The default is `medium`. With `fit`, the panel is as wide as its content and wraps at the `large` width, which suits a short text without a title. On small screens the panel opens as a tray that spans the screen, whatever the size.

### Dedicated components to use instead

A popover can hold anything, which makes it easy to reach for. Most needs already have a more specific component.

| Need                                                                      | Use instead                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Information most people need to finish their task                         | Show it on the page                                                                    |
| Work people keep open beside the page, like a record's details or filters | [Drawer](/components/overlay/drawer)                                                   |
| Content with several sections, steps or its own navigation                | A page of its own                                                                      |
| A list of commands that run and close                                     | [Menu](/components/overlay/menu)                                                       |
| Context or overflow actions behind a "⋮" button                           | [ActionMenu](/components/overlay/action-menu)                                          |
| Choosing one or more values for a form                                    | [Select](/components/form/select)                                                      |
| Typing to pick one value, with nothing else to do on the rows             | [Autocomplete](/components/form/autocomplete) or [ComboBox](/components/form/combobox) |
| Entering several tags                                                     | [TagField](/components/form/tag-field)                                                 |
| A short, passive hint on hover                                            | [Tooltip](/components/overlay/tooltip)                                                 |
| A "?" or "i" help button                                                  | [ContextualHelp](/components/overlay/contextual-help)                                  |
| Required input, a decision, a warning or a destructive confirmation       | [Dialog](/components/overlay/dialog)                                                   |
| A system message without a trigger                                        | [Toast](/components/overlay/toast)                                                     |

### Things to avoid

- **Don't nest popovers.** Opening a popover from inside another one is hard to follow and hard to dismiss. Field dropdowns, like a Select in a form, and the action menus of list rows are fine.
- **Don't hide what people need.** Essential information, required input and warnings belong on the page or in a Dialog. People can miss a popover or close it by accident.
- **Don't make a popover scroll, or let it grow into a page.** When the content needs scrolling, several sections, tabs or steps, or more room than the `large` size, use a Drawer, a Dialog or a page.
- **Don't open a popover on hover or when the page loads.** It opens when people ask for it by pressing its trigger. For a hint on hover, use a Tooltip.
- **Don't repeat what is already on the page.** A popover should add information, not copy it.
- **Don't open a popover from the primary action.** Popovers carry secondary and contextual content.

## Accessibility

The panel is a dialog. A `<Title>` inside `<Popover>` becomes its accessible name automatically. When the panel has no visible title, give `<Popover>` an `aria-label`. An icon-only trigger needs an `aria-label` too.

The trigger exposes whether the panel is open. When the panel opens, focus moves into it. Tab moves through the controls inside, and Escape closes the panel and returns focus to the trigger. Set `keyboardDismissable={false}` on `<Popover.Trigger>` only when there is another clear way out, because a panel the keyboard can't leave is a barrier.
